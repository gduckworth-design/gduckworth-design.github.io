PROCESS DESIGN


Project development and the associated process design, will normally progress through a series of stages where the projects viability, based on the available definition at each stage, is assessed to confirm it is sufficiently financially robust to justify the expense of progressing to the next stage.

Following is a list of the stages that the development of a project may typically progress through, the cost and information required for each stage increasing significantly as the project progresses:

  • ⬥   laboratory investigation
  • ⬥   Scoping Study
  • ⬥   Preliminary Feasibility Study
  • ⬥   Feasibility Study
  • ⬥   Detailed Design and Construction

    • A problem that will be encountered, especially for new projects, is that the information available for design in the early stages will be limited or even non-existent. Laboratory and pilot scale investigations may be necessary as a first step. A second problem is that to arrive at the capital and operating cost estimates that are necessary to assess viability, equipment will need to be selected, sized and priced, but key design criteria may not be available, or may be too expensive to justify the cost of obtaining. A third problem that the engineer may encounter is that the resources available within his/her organisation are strictly limited but the costs of external resourcing cannot be justified.

    This section of the web page is not intended to be a Process Design Manual. Its purpose is to present a number of design tips, suggested approaches and shortcut methods that may assist the process engineer in various areas of process design, checking of vendor equipment offerings and equipment rating. As always the final assessment of validity is the responsibility of the individual engineer undertaking the calculations.
